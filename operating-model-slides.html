The industry context
Four eras — and why the fourth changes everything
The utility industry has been through three genuine transformations — each one a direct response to the failure of the last. A fourth is happening now. Where you are on this journey determines what you need to build next.
Era 1
The Era of Transfer
Customers routed, transferred, and waited. Billing agents handled billing. Metering agents handled metering. If a problem crossed a boundary — and most do — you got passed on. The hierarchy was tall, approval chains long, empowerment low. The contact centre absorbed volume. It was never designed to solve problems, and it wasn't measured on whether problems got solved.
Measured by
Cost per call · AHT · Utilisation
Era 2
The Era of Deflection
The obvious response to too many calls was fewer channels. Web portals arrived. The industry called it digital transformation. But the underlying model didn't change — the goal was still to manage volume, not to serve customers better. Customers who couldn't navigate a self-serve portal called anyway, having already spent twenty minutes failing online first. A lot of utilities are still here.
Measured by
Deflection rate · Digital adoption · Cost per contact
Era 3
The Era of Ownership
The shift that changed the customer experience wasn't digital channels — it was accountability. One person. One customer. End-to-end. No handoff, no transfer. Someone who owns your problem until it's solved. Building this required a different kind of organisation: flatter, faster, more trusted. Ownership runs both ways — an agent accountable for your problem, and for customers who want to manage their own account, the genuine ability to do so. Not deflection. Real agency.
Measured by
FCR · NPS · Customer effort · Self-serve completion
Era 4
The Era of Impact
AI doesn't make the contact centre faster. It makes asking "how many contacts are we handling?" the wrong question. Problems fixed before they become calls. Solutions offered before they're requested. AI as operational capability — not IT infrastructure. The measure is outcomes delivered, not interactions handled. Not a contact centre that has been automated. A utility that genuinely serves its customers, at scale, continuously.
Measured by
Outcomes delivered · Human leverage · Proactive engagement
The goal is impact at scale — not deflection
The right question is no longer "how do we reduce interactions?" It is "how do we maximise the positive impact of every one — and then have more of them?" Total Positive Impact = the impact of each interaction × the volume of interactions, delivered across AI and humans alike.
The destination
Are these the questions you ask your Operations Director?
Handle time. Occupancy. Cost per contact. These are the metrics of a structure built to absorb volume. The destination measures something different entirely.
Today — the contact centre scorecard

Is agent occupancy above target?

Stage 4+
Stage 4 — a retail business owner's scorecard
How many customers are at risk of leaving right now?
What is the lifetime value of our most loyal segment?
How many customers did we save before they could complain?
Where is debt heading — and who can we still intervene for?
Are we growing revenue per customer, or just managing volume?
At Stage 5, the role at the top is called Chief Outcome Officer. Accountable for churn rate, lifetime value, debt resolution — not AHT, occupancy, or cost per contact. Is there a world where your Ops Director holds that brief? There is.
